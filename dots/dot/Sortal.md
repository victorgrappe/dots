According to the _[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Encyclopedia_of_Philosophy "Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy")_, the sortal/nonsortal distinction can be characterized in at least six different ways. It is said that a sortal:[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sortal#cite_note-4)

- gives a criterion for counting the items of that kind
- gives a criterion of identity and non-identity among items of that kind
- gives a criterion for the continued existence of an item of that kind
- answers the question "what is it?" for things of that kind
- specifies the essence of things of that kind
- does not apply to parts of things of that kind



**1. A criterion for counting.** "How many _horses_ are in the field?" has a determinate answer, say four. "How many _brown things_ are in the field?" doesn't: each horse, its mane, its legs, a patch of mud, and a fence post could all count, and there's no principled stopping point. "Horse" supplies a unit; "brown" doesn't.

**2. A criterion of identity and non-identity.** Two books sit on a shelf. Are they the same _book_? It depends on the sortal. As _copies_ (physical objects), they're two. As _works_ (say, both are _Hamlet_), they're one. The sortal tells you what "same" means. "Paper thing" gives no answer, since it doesn't say whether to compare works, copies, or pages.

**3. A criterion for continued existence.** A caterpillar becomes a butterfly. Under the sortal _organism_ (or the species name), one thing survives the change. Under _caterpillar_, the caterpillar ceases to exist. Similarly, if you melt a bronze _statue_, the statue is destroyed, but the _lump of bronze_ survives. Each sortal sets its own conditions for when the thing ends.

**4. Answers "what is it?"** Pointing at Fido, "It's a _dog_" is a proper answer to "what is it?" Saying "It's _furry_," "It's _heavy_," or "It's _asleep_" describes the thing but doesn't say what it is.

**5. Specifies the essence.** Socrates is essentially a _human being_. He couldn't have existed without being one. He is only accidentally a _philosopher_, _snub-nosed_, or _Athenian_, since he could have lacked any of these and still been Socrates. On this criterion, only the first is a sortal in the strict sense. This is where substance sortals ("human") part ways with phase sortals ("philosopher," "child").

**6. Does not apply to parts.** A _cat_'s leg is not a cat, and half a cat is not a cat. But half a lump of _gold_ is still gold, and part of a _red_ surface is still red. Mass terms and adjectives apply to parts ("dissectively"); sortals don't. This is closely tied to counting: because the parts of a cat aren't cats, cats don't overlap in a way that confuses the count.
